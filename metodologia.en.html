Methodology

This page explains how the data are handled: which territory is included, what is counted, how parties are grouped into political blocs and how elections in Hegoalde and Iparralde are combined on one map. Where the data come from is on the Sources page.

Territory

  • Hegoalde (the Spanish side): the municipalities of Araba, Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa and Navarre. New municipalities appear with their own boundaries (for example Usansolo, separated from Galdakao in 2024).
  • Trebiñu (Condado de Treviño and La Puebla de Arganzón): only in local elections, and only in the total for the whole Basque Country; it is not counted within Araba.
  • Iparralde (the French side): the 158 municipalities of the Basque Country Agglomeration plus Eskiula (Zuberoa): 159 municipalities. They can also be viewed by province (Lapurdi, Behe Nafarroa, Zuberoa) and by pole of the Agglomeration.
  • Smallest units: census sections in Hegoalde; polling stations in Iparralde (in larger municipalities). Iparralde also has the legislative constituencies (4th, 5th and 6th) and cantons.

What is counted

  • Percentages are of votes cast for candidates, excluding blank and invalid votes. Turnout = voters / registered electorate.
  • For French elections, the first round only.
  • The vote of Spanish citizens living abroad (CERA) is not tied to any municipality and is not included in the totals.
  • Open lists: in Hegoalde municipalities under 250 inhabitants and Iparralde municipalities under 1,000 (until 2020), people vote for individuals. In Hegoalde, each list gets the votes of its most-voted candidate; in Iparralde, all votes are grouped in a list called «Hautagai libreak» (independent candidates).

Political blocs

In Hegoalde, each party is classified by its acronym, with fixed rules and manual corrections. Iparralde has no party acronyms: the starting point is the political label («nuance») given by the prefecture, and local lists are reviewed by hand.

  • Timeline: at each date, each area shows its latest elections up to that date. The «Today» view combines the latest in each area.
  • Changes (▲ ▼): compared with the previous equivalent election in each area. For the whole Basque Country, only when every area has a previous one.

  • The tooltip (mouse over an area) only lists candidates within 10 points of the leader.

Areas by knowledge of Basque

The map can also group municipalities into three areas by their share of Basque speakers: Basque-speaking area (over 50%), mixed area (30–50%) and Spanish/French-speaking area (under 30%). This is knowledge, not use: the Sociolinguistic Survey has no data by municipality. In the BAC, each municipality's figure from the 2021 census (Eustat); in Navarre, for now, the figure for each language zone (Nastat); in Iparralde, the survey's three areas (BAM, Lapurdi without BAM, Lower Navarre and Zuberoa).

Census sections and areas without data

  • Spain's statistics office (INE) changes census sections almost every year (splitting, merging and renumbering them). Each election is drawn with the year's map that best fits its sections.
  • Some new sections still voted as part of the old one (the electoral roll closes earlier). They are drawn with the data of the section they voted with, and the tooltip says so.
  • In small villages without data by section, the sections show the municipality's result.
  • Hatched areas have no data, and the tooltip gives the reason: the election was not held there; in the 2011 departmental election only half of the cantons voted; or the official source has no results (Navarrese municipalities with no candidates).

Seats

  • Basque Parliament and General Assemblies: official data from the Basque Government.
  • Spanish Congress: official data from the Spanish Ministry of the Interior (Hegoalde's deputies).
  • Parliament of Navarre: calculated with the D'Hondt method (50 seats, 3% threshold); they match the official results for 2011, 2015, 2019 and 2023.
  • Councillors: those of each municipality. In Iparralde, those of municipalities with a second round are missing.
  • There are no seats for Iparralde elections decided in the second round, nor for the European Parliament (single constituency).

Basque Federal Parliament

This is a simulation: what a parliament for the whole Basque Country would look like with the votes from the Regional map. 220 seats split by province: Bizkaia 60, Navarre 45, Gipuzkoa 40, Araba 25, Lapurdi 25, Behe Nafarroa 15 and Zuberoa 10. The D'Hondt method is applied in each province with a 3% threshold; an absolute majority is 111 seats.

Local / provincial gap

Local elections are compared with their provincial-level counterpart in each area: General Assemblies in the BAC and Parliament in Navarre (both on the same day as local elections: 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023), and cantonal (departmental) elections in Iparralde, the nearest in time (2014 ↔ 2015, 2020 ↔ 2021, 2026 ↔ 2021). For each party or bloc: % in local − % in the counterpart. «Total» is the share of the vote that changes (half the sum of the absolute differences).

Local lists count as a single group. Areas where almost all the local vote (95% or more) went to unclassified lists cannot be compared and are hatched: this is the case in most Iparralde villages, whose local lists have no party label. Bayonne (Baiona), Anglet (Angelu) and Boucau (Bokale) are compared as a single unit (added together in both elections): the cantons split Bayonne and Anglet, so the cantonal vote cannot be separated by municipality.

Known limitations

  • In Iparralde, the official French labels do not always tell EH Bai and EAJ-PNB apart; we are reviewing candidates by hand.
  • In some Iparralde elections a large share of the vote is unclassified («Others»).
  • 2022-2024 legislative elections: since NUPES and NFP are in the state-wide left, the Socialist Party barely appears in Iparralde.
  • Comparisons for the whole Basque Country combine different systems and dates; they show trends, not a single result.

Checks

Every time the data are built, automatic checks run: vote totals, turnout, sections against municipalities, unclassified votes and Navarre's seats against the official results. If you spot an error, please write to kontaktua@hautesbehatokia.eus.

Hegoalde results are also reconciled with the official figures. Our totals for each territory, plus the vote of residents abroad (CERA, which the map does not assign to municipalities), are compared with the totals from the Spanish Ministry of the Interior and the Basque Government. From 2011 on, the differences are small (under 0.5 % in the totals) and come from discrepancies between the official files themselves. There are two exceptions. In the 2014 European election the Ministry's municipal file is incomplete, so municipalities were computed by adding up their polling stations; Arruazu, Etaiu and Garaioa have no complete results and are shown hatched. In the 2023 Juntas Generales the per-station file had no votes for PH, which were computed from the votes for lists.
